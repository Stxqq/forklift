Forklift

A small forklift robot works a warehouse. It plans a route down the one-way aisles, lifts a package out of its bay, reads the QR label and drops it at the right dock, and a delivery log checks every step against the order. The scan is real: the label is decoded from pixels, smudges and all. Watch it, dispatch a few, or drive one.

    How it works

    Every 1/60 s each robot moves one fixed step. What it does with that step comes from a short list of jobs, and every job is the same five stages.

    1. 01An order names a package, the bay the records say it's in, and a dock. In Watch they come from the stock records; in Dispatch from your clicks.
    2. 02A* plans a route along the one-way lanes. A corner costs extra, because the robot has to stop and turn on the spot.
    3. 03The robot reserves cells ahead of it and only drives into cells it holds. A crossing is taken together with the cell after it, so nobody stops in the middle of one.
    4. 04At the bay it turns, raises the fork to the shelf, reaches in and lifts. An empty bay is reported missing.
    5. 05The scanner renders what it sees as pixels, finds the code and decodes it, Reed–Solomon and all. A wrong label goes back on the shelf; two failed reads flag the bay for a person.

    Who checks what

    The delivery log doesn't trust the robot. Each line is worked out from what the sensors recorded: what the fork found, what the scanner decoded and what was set down at which dock, against what the order asked for. About one box in twenty sits in the wrong bay or isn't there at all, and one in five has a smudged or torn label, so the log has something to catch.

    The numbers

    Headless runs in Node, half an hour of warehouse time each, on 8 seeds.
